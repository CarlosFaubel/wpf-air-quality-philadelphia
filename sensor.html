Air Quality Metrics

What you're looking at

Your sensor measures six key indicators of indoor air quality, updated every 15 minutes. Here's what each one means for your health and comfort.

PM2.5

μg/m3 · Fine Particles

Tiny particles smaller than 2.5 micrometers that can travel deep into your lungs. Sources include smoke, dust, and vehicle exhaust. Good: below 12 μg/m3.

CO₂

ppm · Carbon Dioxide

A key marker of ventilation. Outdoor air is ~420 ppm. High indoor levels (above 1,000 ppm) can cause drowsiness and reduced focus. Open a window if it rises.

Temperature

°C · Thermal Comfort

Your indoor air temperature. Extreme heat and cold both affect health — especially for children and older adults. Comfort range: 18–26 °C.

Humidity

% RH · Relative Humidity

Too dry and you get irritated airways; too humid and mold and dust mites thrive. Ideal range: 30–50% RH.

VOC

ppm · Volatile Organic Compounds

Gases released from cleaning products, paints, furniture, and air fresheners. Long-term exposure can affect respiratory health. Lower is always better.

Formaldehyde

μg/m3 · Common Indoor Pollutant

Found in building materials, wood products, and some fabrics. Even low levels can irritate eyes, nose, and throat. Lower is always better.

Air Purification

Your Corsi-Rosenthal Box

The Corsi-Rosenthal Box is a low-cost, high-efficiency DIY air purifier built from box fans and MERV-13 filters. It was designed to reduce indoor PM2.5 concentrations — the same particles your sensor monitors — by up to 70%. More information about how to use and maintain your unit is coming soon.

About this study

This project is led by Drexel University's Department of Civil, Architectural and Environmental Engineering, funded by the William Penn Foundation. We are working with communities in four Philadelphia neighborhoods — Point Breeze, Grays Ferry, Haddington, and Kingsessing — to monitor indoor air quality and reduce exposure to PM2.5 and other pollutants.

Your participation directly contributes to research that will help improve air quality across Philadelphia's environmental justice communities.
